Four moves. That's the lot.

How it works
  1. 1

    Drag

    Drag any window onto another window's title bar. Hold a key first if you'd rather grouping only happens on purpose.

  2. 2

    Snap

    A tab bar appears above them and the windows share one frame. Move or resize one, and the rest follow.

  3. 3

    Switch

    Click a tab, or press ⌃⌥⇥, to bring that window to the front. Drag a tab away to pull it out again.

  4. 4

    Save

    Right-click a tab and choose Save Group. Restore it later, apps and all, from the menu bar or a launcher in your Dock.

And everything around it.

FeaturesStage ManagerDockShortcuts

Same app, one group

Auto-tab gathers an app's open windows into one group and adds its new windows as tabs. Turn it on for every app, or only the ones you choose. Press ⌘N in a grouped window and the new window opens as a tab.

One item in Stage Manager

A group shows as one item in the Stage Manager strip, and minimising it sends the whole group there together. Bring it back and every tab comes with it.

A Dock icon for your groups

Show iGroup in the Dock and its icon combines the front group's apps and shows how many groups are open. Its menu lists every group and saved grouping.

Rules and app lists

Match windows by app and title text, then always tab the matches together or never group them. Keep a list of apps iGroup should never touch, or the only apps it should.

Keyboard shortcuts

⌃⌥⇥ next tab, ⌃⌥⇧⇥ previous, ⌃⌥W close, ⌃⌥U ungroup and ⌃⌥R rename. Change any of them in Settings.

One kind of tab

Use iGroup tabs instead of native tabs in Finder and TextEdit. Turn it off and your old settings come back.
